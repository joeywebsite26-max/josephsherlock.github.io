Politricks

Published in PNAS Nexus
Context
Misinformation threatens democracy and climate action, eroding trust and amplifying polarization.
Problem
Few studies compare active (games) and passive (guides) learning in helping citizens discern manipulative content.
Intervention
We built Politricks, an interactive game, alongside a passive inoculation guide, each teaching three manipulation tactics: emotional storytelling, conformity, and conspiracy theories.
Outcome
Active and passive tools are comparably effective, scalable strategies for building resilience against misinformation.

Operational Transparency in Elections

Preprint · under review at Nature Human Behaviour
Context
Democracies face declining trust fuelled by polarization, misinformation, and hidden election processes.
Problem
Voters often misinterpret how elections work, leading to scepticism and disengagement.
Intervention
Applying behavioural insights to increase operational transparency, redesign ballots for usability, and work with election officials to test communication and procedural reforms.
Outcome
Evidence-based, incremental interventions make democratic systems more understandable and trustworthy, reducing misinformation’s impact and strengthening confidence in elections.

COVID-19 Vaccination in Ghana

Published in PLOS ONE
Context
Ghana faced vaccine hesitancy despite availability, with uptake shaped by social and cultural factors.
Problem
Traditional drivers such as provider recommendations were unexpectedly less predictive; family and religious leaders influenced decisions far more.
Intervention
A two-part study surveyed 1,494 people and tested behaviourally informed message frames (fear, altruism, social norms) delivered through trusted messengers.
Outcome
Tailoring strategies to leverage social trust and context-specific nudges can increase vaccine uptake in Ghana.
